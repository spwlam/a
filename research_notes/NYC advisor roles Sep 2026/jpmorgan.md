## Do these roles require Series 7 and Series 66 licenses?

### Takeaway
Yes — the Private Client Advisor role at J.P. Morgan Wealth Management requires both Series 7 and Series 66 (or 63/65) licenses, plus a Life, Health and Long Term Care Insurance license; candidates without existing licenses may be hired and required to obtain them within a defined timeframe.

### Cited Findings
- "A valid and active Series 66 (63/65) license is required or must be obtained within 60 days of starting in the role as a condition of employment." — referenced in search result snippet from job listing aggregators (ZipRecruiter / National Postdoc Association career board).
- "A valid and active Life, Health and Long Term Care Insurance license is required or must be obtained within 60 days of starting." — same source as above.
- "Unlicensed individuals or those without a Series 7 will hold the Financial Advisor in Licensing title until licensing is obtained within required timeframe" — indicating Series 7 is a prerequisite for the full title but candidates can be hired without it. — search result snippet from ZipRecruiter aggregator.
- "To thrive as a Private Client Advisor at JP Morgan, you need… FINRA licenses (such as Series 7 and 66)." — [ZipRecruiter listing search](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Private-Client-Advisor-Jp-Morgan/-in-New-York,NY)
- The 2026 Global Private Bank Advisor Program is an entry-level track that presumably starts candidates on the licensing path, but specifics were not accessible.

### Inferences
- JPMorgan clearly prefers Series 7 + Series 66 licensed candidates but will hire unlicensed individuals under a provisional "Financial Advisor in Licensing" title and require them to obtain licenses within ~60 days.
- This mirrors industry norms for branch-based wealth advisory roles at large wirehouses/banks.

### Gaps
- Series 7 requirement was described indirectly (by inference from "Financial Advisor in Licensing" title language) rather than a direct explicit statement in a primary source; could not access the full job description page directly.

## Are these roles genuinely advisory-track (not just basic banking or teller roles)?

### Takeaway
Yes — the Private Client Advisor title is a distinct, investment-advisory-focused role explicitly differentiated from branch banking/teller work; it sits within J.P. Morgan Wealth Management (not Chase Retail Banking) and serves affluent clients with investable assets typically starting at $150,000+.

### Cited Findings
- "The branch-based Private Client Advisor offers comprehensive financial planning and advice to individuals and families." Advisors "conduct investment reviews with clients to validate clients' current investment objectives and prepare recommendations of appropriate investment products." — search result snippets from [Chase / ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Private-Client-Advisor-Jp-Morgan/-in-New-York,NY)
- J.P. Morgan Private Client is "designed for clients who intend to invest $150,000 or more" — dedicated support from "a Chase Private Client banker and a J.P. Morgan advisor, helping you keep your banking, lending, and investing in sync." — [Chase Private Client product page](https://www.chase.com/personal/investments/private-client)
- "Demonstrated success managing affluent client relationships ($5MM+ investable assets preferred)" is listed as a job requirement in some advisor-level postings. — search result snippet.
- "Bankers in your Chase branch refer qualified investors to you — freeing you up to focus on building deep relationships with your clients." — indicates a referral model where PCAs receive warm leads from branch bankers, confirming advisory-not-teller function. — search snippet from [Chase career page](https://www.chase.com/personal/investments/advisor/career)
- "Focus on relationship management with a deep understanding of financial markets… embracing digital innovations to help clients become more digitally confident." — job description language confirming advisory nature.
- "Expand your abilities in the Advisor Growth Program, which provides access to personalized coaching, thought leaders and peer groups." — advisory development infrastructure. — search snippet.
- The role is housed under "J.P. Morgan Wealth Management" branding (not Chase Retail), consistent with advisory positioning. — [J.P. Morgan Wealth Management careers page](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/careers/explore-opportunities/programs/wealth-management-fulltime-financial-advisor-development-program)

Whether it qualifies as "advisory-track" depends on the specific posting; some listings emphasize investment product recommendations alongside day-to-day banking.

## What is the compensation structure — salary, bonus, commissions?

### Takeaway
Private Client Advisors at J.P. Morgan receive a base salary plus a monthly incentive tied to revenue generation and a Net New Money (AUM-based) annual award — total compensation ranges from approximately $144K (25th percentile) to $269K (75th percentile) annually, with top earners exceeding $350K.

### Cited Findings
- "Comprehensive compensation plan, including a competitive base salary and incentive structure, subject to the Private Client Advisor Incentive Compensation Plan." — [Chase career page / search snippet](https://www.chase.com/personal/investments/advisor/career)
- "Compensation includes a base salary, monthly incentives based on revenues and a Net New Money award annually." — search snippet from ZipRecruiter / salary aggregator.
- "Investment professionals are paid a salary and are eligible for a discretionary bonus… Discretionary bonuses are not related directly to the sale of any specific product or services." — primary source: [J.P. Morgan Investment Services Compensation and Conflicts disclosure](https://investment.jpmorgan.com/investment-services/compensation-and-conflicts)
- Glassdoor data (66 salaries submitted): estimated average total pay of $191,878/year; 25th–75th percentile range $143,908–$268,629; 90th percentile ~$351,136. — [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salary/J-P-Morgan-Private-Client-Advisor-Salaries-E145_D_KO11,33.htm)
- ZipRecruiter hourly data (as of July 24, 2026): average $28.37/hour (~$59K/year), range $20.24–$32.60/hour — this likely reflects lower-level or Part-time entries and does NOT represent total compensation for the advisory role; inconsistent with Glassdoor. — [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/co/jp-morgan/Jobs/Private-Client-Advisor/-in-New-York,NY)
- Average Financial Advisor at J.P. Morgan in NYC: $159,473/year estimate (appears to reflect base or partial comp). — Glassdoor NYC-specific data via search snippet.
- Financial Advisor Development Program (entry-level track): average base ~$99K/year + ~$16K average additional pay = ~$115K total average. Typical range $109,791–$189,350. — [Glassdoor FADP salary](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salary/JPMorganChase-Financial-Advisor-Development-Program-Salaries-E5224839_D_KO14,51.htm)
- JPMorgan Chase Private Banker (different role): average base salary $215K — [6figr.com via search snippet](https://6figr.com/us/salary/jpmorgan-chase--private-banker)

### Inferences
- The "monthly incentive based on revenues" is a form of production-based pay, functionally akin to commission, though J.P. Morgan does not describe it as a traditional commission to avoid conflicts-of-interest disclosure triggers.
- The ZipRecruiter hourly figure ($28/hr) is not representative of the advisory-track PCA role; it likely aggregates lower-level or part-time branch associate data and should be disregarded.
- Total comp for an established NYC-based Private Client Advisor is credibly in the $150K–$270K range, with top performers earning $350K+.
- Entry-level candidates entering via the Financial Advisor Development Program should expect $100K–$190K total comp range while building their book.

### Gaps
- No publicly accessible breakdown of exact base salary vs. incentive split (e.g., 60% base / 40% variable) for the PCA role was found.
- Commission or "Net New Money" award formula specifics were not publicly documented.
- No 2026-specific compensation updates (pay band adjustments) were found; Glassdoor data reflects historical submissions.
